01 — The claim, up front

A pool of subscription coding agents, scheduled by arithmetic instead of hope, covers 100% of around-the-clock development demand at $319/month — where the same money spent as two plain subscriptions covers 63%. The gap is not model quality. It is scheduling.

Framed honestly: this is proven by simulation — a 70-test contract suite over a discrete-event backtest of demand traces — not a mathematical theorem about all futures.

04 — What the scheduler does

Five rules, and why it holds at 100%.

  1. Meters before model calls

    Every lane's hourly, daily and weekly burn is checked before work starts. A refused task costs zero tokens.

  2. Leak stops same-day

    A runaway agent — 13%/hr with no output was the first catch — is parked automatically. 2%/hour audits, 4% and 7% soft stops, 12% and 15% hard stops.

  3. Overnight unattended missions

    Headless builder plus an independent critic per phase; every morning has a report of done / evidence / blocked. 45 logged episodes, measured quality 0.87–0.92 on the free lane.

  4. Warm sessions, bounded context

    Compaction points solved from the provider's own price ratio — K* = b + √(2·(f/c)·(b+R)·d) — cutting a measured burn by 42%.

  5. Degradation by design

    Lanes fail in a ladder: Claude → Codex + free → free + GLM → Kimi. The queue never dies; routing just gets cheaper and slower.
