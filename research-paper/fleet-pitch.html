The claim, up front

A pool of subscription coding agents, scheduled by arithmetic instead of hope, covers 100% of around-the-clock development demand at $319/month — where the same money spent as two plain subscriptions covers 63%. The gap is not model quality. It is scheduling.

Framed honestly: this is proven by simulation — a 70-test contract suite over a discrete-event backtest of demand traces — not a mathematical theorem about all futures.

Any single agent seat hits weekly quota ceilings, sleeps while you sleep, and burns its best hours on grind. What the scheduler does (and why it holds at 100%)

  1. Meters before model calls. Every lane's hourly/daily/weekly burn is checked before work starts; a refused task costs zero tokens.
  2. Leak stops same-day. A runaway agent (13%/hr with no output was the first catch) is parked automatically; 2%/hour audits, 4%/7% soft stops, 12%/15% hard stops.
  3. Overnight unattended missions. Headless builder + independent critic per phase; every morning has a report of done/evidence/blocked — 45 logged episodes, measured quality 0.87–0.92 on the free lane.
  4. Warm sessions, bounded context. Compaction points solved from the provider's own price ratio (K* = b + √(2·(f/c)·(b+R)·d)), cutting a measured burn by 42%.
  5. Degradation by design. Lanes fail in a ladder (Claude → Codex+free → free+GLM → Kimi); the queue never dies, routing just gets cheaper and slower.

What it costs to be honest
